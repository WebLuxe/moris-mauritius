Summer · Nov–Apr

Hot beach days

The warmest sea and the best snorkelling, with short tropical showers. January to March is cyclone season, so book flexible plans and check forecasts.

Winter · May–Oct

Hiking & exploring

Cooler, drier and breezier, which makes it ideal for hikes and road trips. September and October are a sweet spot of sunshine and pleasant temperatures.

Essentials

Good to know

The practical stuff, at a glance.

Currency

Mauritian rupee (MUR)

Cards are widely accepted in towns and hotels. Carry cash for markets and street food.

Languages

English, French & Kreol

English is the official language. French and Mauritian Kreol are spoken everywhere.

Time zone

UTC+4

No daylight saving, so the time difference changes with your home country's seasons.

Getting around

Drive on the left

Renting a car gives the most freedom. Taxis are easy to find, and buses and the Metro Express connect the main towns.

Arriving

One international airport

Flights land at SSR International in the south-east, about an hour from most resorts.

Entry

Check your passport

Many nationalities can visit visa-free for short stays. Always check the current official requirements before you book.

Quick answers

A week lets you see the highlights without rushing. With 10 days or more you can split your stay between two coasts, for example west for sunsets and east for lagoons.

North for restaurants and nightlife, west for sunsets and calm water, east for wide lagoons and a cooler breeze, and south for wild scenery and quiet.

Inside the reef, lagoons are generally calm, but always follow local flags and advice. Avoid swimming on the reef-less south coast and around river mouths after heavy rain.

It isn't essential, as taxis and tours cover most sights, but a car makes day trips much easier. Remember that traffic drives on the left.

Light clothes, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, water shoes for rocky beaches, a light rain jacket, and something modest to cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples.
